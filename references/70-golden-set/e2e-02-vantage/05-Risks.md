# ⚠️ Section 5. Risks

Every risk carries a trigger — the observable sign that it has materialised. A risk without one is not noticed in time.

| Risk | Likelihood / impact | Mitigation | Owner | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R1 · The public commitment cannot be met in the unit it was stated in** | high / high | restate the metric with the CPO before the board pack is drafted; report the new unit from the first quarter | CPO | the board pack is drafted using the call-share number |
| **R2 · Narrowing the assistant is read as a rollback** | high / medium | publish the perimeter as a guarantee rather than a restriction; produce the outside-perimeter number and the routing before the announcement, not after | Assistant PM | the first executive complaint that "it used to answer that" |
| **R3 · The twelve definitions live on the goodwill of two borrowed people** | medium / high | the metric owners get a weekly decision slot with an explicit time budget; overrunning it is treated as a platform problem, not a personal one | Head of Analytics | the slot overruns two weeks running, or a definition waits more than 10 days |
| **R4 · Squad autonomy rejects central definitions** | medium / high | the mandate covers twelve metrics only, and those twelve are the ones the squads' own targets are graded on | Head of Data Platform | a squad ships a conflicting definition after the central one is published |
| **R5 · The core outside the perimeter stays wrong, and the assistant looks trustworthy anyway** | medium / high | provenance on every answer; the perimeter is visible in the interface, not only in the policy | Assistant PM | an in-perimeter answer is quoted for a question that was outside it |
| **R6 · The sponsor changes** | medium / high | the restated commitment is documented and agreed in writing, not verbally; the strategy is written to survive its author | Head of Analytics | the CPO's remit changes, or the investor-day narrative is rewritten without us |
| **R7 · A third of the resource is lost mid-year** | medium / high | the freeze list below, published in advance | Head of Data Platform | the funding round slips past Q2 |
| **R8 · A regulated-data question stops the assistant** | low / high | I6 gives it its own identity and an audit trail before the next access review | Data Platform lead | any request for a per-user read log |

## What is not on this list, and why

**"Insufficient funding" and "resistance to change"** are deliberately absent. Neither has a trigger or an owner, and a register that carries them reads as a formality. R3, R4 and R7 say the same things in a form somebody can act on.

## The freeze list

If a third of the resource is lost, this is the order — published now, not drawn up in the week of the cut.

| Order | What dies | Why it can |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **S5 · dashboard hygiene** entirely | 912 dashboards are a real problem and the cheapest one to postpone |
| 2 | **Expansion of the assistant's perimeter beyond perimeter 1** | perimeter 1 delivers the guarantee; everything past it is improvement |
| 3 | **Trusted core domains 5–6** | domain 4 is the one the perimeter needs |
| 4 | **Metric definitions 9–12** | scope narrows to the four board metrics |

**Never frozen:** the golden set and the accuracy baseline (I1), and the restated commitment (I2). Both are cheap, and without them nothing else in this document can be defended in four months. If the resource is so short that even these go, the honest move is to say the commitment will not be met — not to keep working quietly.
